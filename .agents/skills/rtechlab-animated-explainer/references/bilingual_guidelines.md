# Bilingual Guidelines: English & Hindi Narration

## 1. Technical Vocabulary Preservation
In Indian technical education, transliterating engineering terms into formal Hindi words (e.g. translating "Router" or "Network Packet") alienates engineers and learners.

### The Preservation Rule:
Always preserve standard industry terms:
- **Keep in English / Hinglish Phonetics**:
  - Router, Switch, Packet, IP Address, Domain Name, Browser, Cache
  - DNS Resolver, Root Server, TLD Server, Authoritative Server
  - Port, Socket, Firewall, Handshake, Latency, Milliseconds, Bandwidth
  - A Record, AAAA Record, CNAME, TTL

## 2. Voice Persona Calibration

| Language | Model Voice | Style / Pitch | Delivery Tone |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **English** | `en-US-ChristopherNeural` or `en-US-GuyNeural` | Pitch: default, Rate: +2% | Confident, punchy, tech-enthusiast pacing. Clear enunciation. |
| **Hindi** | `hi-IN-MadhurNeural` | Pitch: default, Rate: +0% | Engaging, pedagogical, conversational Indian tech-educator cadence. |

## 3. Subtitle Formatting Rules
- Vertical Safe Zone: Top `18%` to `80%` of screen. Keep subtitles within `Y = 1380px` to `1560px` so they sit right above social media caption overlays.
- Max characters per line: 36 characters.
- Font styling: `Plus Jakarta Sans` or `Inter`, SemiBold 700, 48px, high contrast white text with dark translucent backdrop (`rgba(8, 13, 26, 0.85)`).
- Subtitle synchronization: Exactly align with audio phrase markers.
