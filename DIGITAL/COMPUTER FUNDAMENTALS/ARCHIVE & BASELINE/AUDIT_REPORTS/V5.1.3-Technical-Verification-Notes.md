## 1. Electrical & Physical Semiconductor Formulations

### 1.1 CMOS Dynamic and Static Power Dissipation
The total power dissipation in complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS) logic is governed by:
$$P_\text{total} = P_\text{dynamic} + P_\text{short-circuit} + P_\text{static}$$
Where:
- **Dynamic Switching Power ($P_\text{dynamic}$):**
  $$P_\text{dynamic} = \alpha \cdot C_L \cdot V_{DD}^2 \cdot f$$
  - $\alpha$: **Switching Activity Factor**, defined specifically as the probability that a power-consuming $0 \to 1$ logic transition occurs in a given clock cycle. Physical derivation: Energy $E = C_L \cdot V_{DD}^2$ is drawn from the power supply $V_{DD}$ only during a $0 \to 1$ transition to charge the load capacitance $C_L$. (During a $1 \to 0$ transition, the stored charge is dissipated to ground without drawing energy from $V_{DD}$). Typical clock and logic values range from $0.1 \le \alpha \le 0.5$.
  - *Alternative Convention Note:* If activity is alternatively defined by counting **total transitions** (both $0 \to 1$ and $1 \to 0$, where $\alpha_\text{total} = 2 \alpha_{0 \to 1}$), the equation requires a $1/2$ factor: $P_\text{dynamic} = \frac{1}{2} \alpha_\text{total} \cdot C_L \cdot V_{DD}^2 \cdot f$. In this curriculum, $\alpha$ strictly denotes the $0 \to 1$ switching probability, ensuring equation and definition are in identical alignment.
  - $C_L$: Total lumped capacitive load of interconnect wiring and fan-out gate inputs.
  - $V_{DD}$: Core power supply voltage.
  - $f$: Clock switching frequency.
- **Static Subthreshold Leakage Power ($P_\text{static}$):**
  $$P_\text{static} = I_\text{leakage} \cdot V_{DD} \propto V_{DD} \cdot e^{-\frac{q V_t}{k T}}$$
- **Dennard Scaling Breakdown:** Robert Dennard's 1974 scaling theory established that as transistor dimensions shrunk by a factor of $k = 1.4$, power density remained constant because supply voltage $V_{DD}$ scaled down proportionally. Around 2005 (below the 90 nm node), $V_{DD}$ could no longer be reduced without triggering excessive subthreshold static leakage ($I_\text{leakage}$), breaking Dennard scaling and capping single-core clock frequencies at $\sim 4\text{--}5\text{ GHz}$.
- **Citation:** Weste & Harris, *CMOS VLSI Design: A Circuits and Systems Perspective* (4th Edition, Section 4.4).

## 2. High-Speed Bus Architectures & Signal Integrity

### 2.1 PCIe 6.0 Physical Signaling & Raw Bandwidth
- **Modulation:** Pulse Amplitude Modulation 4-Level (PAM4, 4 voltage levels encoding 2 bits per baud).
- **Symbol Rate:** 32 GBaud operating at 64 GT/s per lane.
- **Raw Simplex Data Rate per Lane:**
  $$\text{Throughput}_\text{lane} = \frac{64\text{ Gbps}}{8\text{ bits/byte}} = \mathbf{8\text{ GB/s per lane per direction (simplex)}}$$
- **x16 Link Maximum Raw Bandwidth:**
  $$\text{Simplex Throughput}_\text{x16} = 16 \times 8\text{ GB/s} = \mathbf{128\text{ GB/s per direction (simplex)}}$$
  $$\text{Bidirectional Raw Maximum}_\text{x16} = 128\text{ GB/s} \times 2 = \mathbf{256\text{ GB/s bidirectional raw maximum}}$$
- **FLIT Mode Framing & Net Payload Qualification:** PCIe 6.0 operates strictly in fixed 256-byte Flow Control Units (FLITs) protected by a 10-byte CRC and 8-byte 2-way interleaved Forward Error Correction (FEC). Net application payload throughput is not a fixed universal figure, but varies based on TLP payload size (64B to 512B+), packet framing overhead, and link-level retry mechanisms. The specification guarantees 128 GB/s simplex and 256 GB/s bidirectional raw maximum link bandwidth across an x16 configuration.
- **Citation:** PCI-SIG PCIe 6.0 Base Specification, Section 3 & 4.

## 3. Memory Hierarchy, DDR5 Refresh Standards & Storage Math

### 3.1 DDR5 Refresh Standards ($t_\text{REFW}$ vs $t_\text{REFI}$) per JEDEC JESD79-5
In DDR5 SDRAM (JESD79-5 / JESD79-5C):
- **1x Refresh Mode (Normal Operating Temperature, $T_\text{CASE} \le 85^\circ\text{C}$):**
  $$t_\text{REFW} = \mathbf{32\text{ ms}}, \quad \text{Refresh Cycles} = 8,192\text{ (8K)}$$
  $$t_\text{REFI} = \frac{t_\text{REFW}}{8192} = \frac{32\text{ ms}}{8192} = \mathbf{3.90625\text{ }\mu\text{s}} \approx \mathbf{3.9\text{ }\mu\text{s}}$$
- **2x Refresh Mode (Elevated Operating Temperature, $85^\circ\text{C} < T_\text{CASE} \le 95^\circ\text{C}$):**
  $$t_\text{REFW} = \mathbf{16\text{ ms}}, \quad \text{Refresh Cycles} = 8,192\text{ (8K)}$$
  $$t_\text{REFI} = \frac{t_\text{REFW}}{8192} = \frac{16\text{ ms}}{8192} = \mathbf{1.953125\text{ }\mu\text{s}} \approx \mathbf{1.95\text{ }\mu\text{s}}$$
- **Density-Specific Refresh Recovery Time ($t_\text{RFC}$) per JEDEC Table 170:**
  Refresh recovery timing is strictly die-density dependent and must not be generalized across DDR5. Per JEDEC JESD79-5 Section 4.14 Table 170:
  - **8Gb Monolithic Die:** $t_\text{RFC1} = \mathbf{195\text{ ns}}$ (all-bank), $t_\text{RFCsb} = \mathbf{115\text{ ns}}$ (same-bank)
  - **16Gb Monolithic Die:** $t_\text{RFC1} = \mathbf{295\text{ ns}}$ (all-bank), $t_\text{RFCsb} = \mathbf{130\text{ ns}}$ (same-bank)
  - **24Gb Monolithic Die:** $t_\text{RFC1} = \mathbf{410\text{ ns}}$ (all-bank), $t_\text{RFCsb} = \mathbf{180\text{ ns}}$ (same-bank)
  - **32Gb Monolithic Die:** $t_\text{RFC1} \ge \mathbf{410\text{ ns}}$ per vendor/revision specification.
- **Architectural Mitigation:** JEDEC introduced Same-Bank Refresh (SBR) to refresh one bank while keeping other bank groups available, and Per-Row Activation Counting (PRAC, JESD79-5C) to manage refresh latency overhead and mitigate Rowhammer vulnerabilities.
- **Legacy Contrast with DDR4 (JESD79-4):** DDR4 utilized $t_\text{REFW} = 64\text{ ms}$ ($t_\text{REFI} = 7.8\text{ }\mu\text{s}$) at normal temperatures and $32\text{ ms}$ ($t_\text{REFI} = 3.9\text{ }\mu\text{s}$) at elevated temperatures. DDR5 halved the window to 32 ms to account for higher nanometer cell charge leakage.
- **Citation:** JEDEC Standard JESD79-5 / JESD79-5C, Section 4.14, Table 169 & Table 170.

### 3.2 Binary vs Decimal Storage Capacity Conversion
- **Decimal 1 Terabyte (SI):**
  $$1\text{ TB} = 10^{12}\text{ bytes} = 1,000,000,000,000\text{ bytes}$$
- **Binary 1 Gibibyte (IEC):**
  $$1\text{ GiB} = 2^{30}\text{ bytes} = 1,073,741,824\text{ bytes}$$
- **Exact Ratio Conversion:**
  $$\text{Capacity in GiB} = \frac{1,000,000,000,000\text{ bytes}}{1,073,741,824\text{ bytes/GiB}} = \mathbf{931.32257\dots\text{ GiB}} \approx \mathbf{931.32\text{ GiB}}$$
- **Citation:** IEC 60027-2 & IEEE Std 1541-2002.

## 4. CPU Microarchitecture & Peak Theoretical FLOPS

### 4.1 Peak Theoretical FLOPS with Dual 512-bit FMA Units
$$\text{Peak Theoretical FLOPS} = N_\text{cores} \times f_\text{clock} \times \text{FLOPs/Cycle per Core}$$
Under the stated dual 512-bit FMA unit assumption per core:
- **Single-Precision (FP32):**
  $$\text{Elements per 512-bit register} = \frac{512\text{ bits}}{32\text{ bits}} = 16\text{ elements}$$
  $$\text{Operations per FMA unit} = 16 \times 2\text{ (multiply + add)} = 32\text{ FP32 FLOPs/unit/cycle}$$
  $$\text{Dual FMA Units per Core} = 32 \times 2 = \mathbf{64\text{ FP32 FLOPs/cycle}}$$
- **Double-Precision (FP64):**
  $$\text{Elements per 512-bit register} = \frac{512\text{ bits}}{64\text{ bits}} = 8\text{ elements}$$
  $$\text{Operations per FMA unit} = 8 \times 2 = 16\text{ FP64 FLOPs/unit/cycle}$$
  $$\text{Dual FMA Units per Core} = 16 \times 2 = \mathbf{32\text{ FP64 FLOPs/cycle}}$$
- **Citation:** IEEE Standard 754-2019 / SPEC CPU2017 Benchmark Rules.

## 5. Digital Logic & Signed Arithmetic Proofs

### 5.1 Signed 2's Complement Overflow Rule Proof
In an $n$-bit signed 2's complement adder:
$$\mathbf{\text{Overflow} = C_\text{in} \oplus C_\text{out} = 1}$$
Where $C_\text{in}$ is carry-in into the most significant sign bit, and $C_\text{out}$ is carry-out from the sign bit.
- Adding two positive operands ($A_{n-1}=0, B_{n-1}=0$) produces overflow iff sum is negative ($S_{n-1}=1$), requiring $C_\text{in}=1$ and $C_\text{out}=0 \implies C_\text{in} \oplus C_\text{out} = 1$.
- Adding two negative operands ($A_{n-1}=1, B_{n-1}=1$) produces overflow iff sum is positive ($S_{n-1}=0$), requiring $C_\text{in}=0$ and $C_\text{out}=1 \implies C_\text{in} \oplus C_\text{out} = 1$.
- Operands of opposite signs cannot overflow ($C_\text{in} \oplus C_\text{out} = 0$).
- **Citation:** Floyd, *Digital Fundamentals* (11th Edition) / Mano & Ciletti.

## 6. Networking & Transport Layer Formulations

### 6.1 Ethernet MTU, Frame Size & TCP MSS
- **Layer 3 MTU:** $\mathbf{1500\text{ bytes}}$.
- **Layer 2 Frame Size:**
  - Untagged Frame: $14\text{B header} + 1500\text{B payload} + 4\text{B FCS} = \mathbf{1518\text{ bytes}}$.
  - IEEE 802.1Q Tagged Frame: $1518\text{B} + 4\text{B VLAN tag} = \mathbf{1522\text{ bytes}}$.
  - Physical Wire Overhead: 8B preamble/SFD + 12B IPG = 20 byte-times.
- **TCP Maximum Segment Size (MSS):**
  - Standard IPv4 without options: $1500 - (20\text{B IP} + 20\text{B TCP}) = \mathbf{1460\text{ bytes}}$.
  - IPv4 with Timestamps option (RFC 7323, +12B): $1500 - 52 = \mathbf{1448\text{ bytes}}$.
  - Standard IPv6 without options: $1500 - (40\text{B IPv6} + 20\text{B TCP}) = \mathbf{1440\text{ bytes}}$.
- **Citation:** IEEE Std 802.3-2022 / RFC 791 / RFC 8200 / RFC 7323.

### 6.2 DNS IP Fragmentation Avoidance (RFC 9715 & DNS Flag Day 2020)
- **Problem:** Oversized DNS-over-UDP payloads trigger IP fragmentation, causing high packet drop rates and security vulnerabilities (RFC 8900).
- **Conservative Baseline (1232 Bytes):** DNS Flag Day 2020 recommendation:
  $$\text{IPv6 Minimum Link MTU (1280B)} - 40\text{B IPv6 header} - 8\text{B UDP header} = \mathbf{1232\text{ bytes}}$$
  Serves as a conservative fragmentation-avoidance recommendation for DNS over UDP, minimizing the likelihood of fragmentation across IPv6/IPv4 paths without claiming an absolute guarantee of zero fragmentation across all non-compliant or unusual network tunnels.
- **Contextual Resolver Ceiling (1400 Bytes):** RFC 9715 (*IP Fragmentation Avoidance in DNS over UDP*, published January 2025 by Fujiwara & Vixie) discusses $\sim 1400\text{ bytes}$ as an operational upper bound deployed by major public DNS resolvers (such as Google Public DNS) to balance transmission efficiency and avoid fragmentation on standard 1500-byte Ethernet links carrying typical tunneling or encapsulation overhead. Neither 1232B nor 1400B is an immutable fixed protocol limit.
- **Truncation (`TC=1`):** Responses exceeding the advertised buffer size trigger fallback to connection-oriented **TCP port 53**.
- **Citation:** RFC 9715 (Jan 2025) / RFC 6891 / DNS Flag Day 2020 Guidelines / RFC 1035.

## 7. Cybersecurity, Cryptography & Standards Verification

### 7.1 NIST Post-Quantum Cryptography FIPS Standards (August 13, 2024)
- **FIPS 203:** Module-Lattice-Based Key-Encapsulation Mechanism (ML-KEM, CRYSTALS-Kyber).
- **FIPS 204:** Module-Lattice-Based Digital Signature Algorithm (ML-DSA, CRYSTALS-Dilithium).
- **FIPS 205:** Stateless Hash-Based Digital Signature Algorithm (SLH-DSA, SPHINCS+).
- **Quantum Threat to Cryptography:** Shor's Algorithm solves prime factorization and discrete logarithms in polynomial time relative to the number of input bits $\log N$, running exponentially faster than known classical algorithms (such as the General Number Field Sieve). This polynomial-time scaling mathematically compromises classical public-key cryptography (RSA, Diffie-Hellman, ECDSA, Ed25519) on cryptographically relevant quantum computers, without relying on or claiming an unverified universal exact asymptotic constant or implementation-specific gate complexity formula. Grover's Algorithm provides quadratic speedup for unstructured search, effectively halving symmetric cipher security (mitigated by doubling key sizes, e.g. AES-256).
- **Citation:** NIST FIPS 203, FIPS 204, FIPS 205 (August 13, 2024) / Peter W. Shor (1994).

### 7.2 NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2 Media Sanitization Standards (September 2025)
- **Scope:** Information Storage Media (ISM).
- **Clear:** Logical sanitization using interface commands; single-pass overwrite is sufficient for magnetic HDDs, but is **NOT universally sufficient for SSDs/flash storage** due to FTL wear-leveling and unmapped blocks.
- **Purge:** Controller-level firmware execution: Cryptographic Erase (CE) or controller-native block-erase sanitize commands (NVMe/ATA Sanitize) for flash storage. **Degaussing is categorized under Purge strictly for suitable legacy magnetic media** (magnetic tapes, magnetic HDDs) and is completely ineffective on solid-state flash media.
- **Destroy:** Physical disintegration, incineration, shredding.
- **Verification vs Validation:** Technical verification confirms software/firmware tool execution without error; organizational validation confirms risk-based data unrecoverability.
- **Citation:** NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2 (September 2025) / IEEE Std 2883-2022.
