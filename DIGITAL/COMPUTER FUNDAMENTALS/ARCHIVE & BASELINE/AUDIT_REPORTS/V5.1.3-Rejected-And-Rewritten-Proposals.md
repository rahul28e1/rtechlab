### Item 1: PCIe 6.0 x16 Bandwidth Formulation & FLIT Mode Net Payload
- **Affected Pages:** Page 10 (Ch 2.7), Page 39 (Q11), Page 45 (Bibliography).
- **Initial Flawed Proposal:** Stated that "PCIe 6.0 operating at 64 GT/s yields ~128 GB/s bidirectional raw bandwidth over an x16 link" and earlier drafts claimed a universal guaranteed "~242 GB/s net bidirectional payload bandwidth."
- **Defect Identified:** Conflated simplex (per direction) bandwidth with bidirectional bandwidth. At 64 GT/s with PAM4 signaling, an x16 link provides $16 \times 8\text{ GB/s} = 128\text{ GB/s}$ per direction (simplex), which yields **256 GB/s bidirectional raw maximum**. Furthermore, presenting ~242 GB/s net payload as a guaranteed universal figure is technically ungrounded because net application payload throughput depends on TLP packet payload size (64B to 512B+), framing overhead, and the mandatory 256-byte FLIT mode structure (10B CRC + 8B FEC).
- **Reviewer Decision:** REJECTED INITIAL 128 GB/S BIDIRECTIONAL CLAIM AND REJECTED UNGROUNDED ~242 GB/S PAYLOAD CLAIM. RETAINED 128 GB/S SIMPLEX AND 256 GB/S BIDIRECTIONAL RAW MAXIMUM; QUALIFIED FLIT MODE OVERHEAD.
- **Approved Rewritten Formulation:** Specify PCIe 6.0 physical layer signaling: operates at 64 GT/s per lane using PAM4 (2 bits per baud across 4 voltage levels), delivering 8 GB/s raw bandwidth per lane per direction. Over a standard x16 bus, this yields approximately **128 GB/s per direction (simplex)** and **256 GB/s bidirectional raw maximum**. Flit mode mandates 256-byte Flow Control Units with CRC and FEC; net application payload throughput depends on packet payload size and framing overhead rather than a fixed universal payload figure.
- **Authoritative Standard:** PCI-SIG PCIe 6.0 Base Specification, Section 3 & 4.

### Item 2: Dual 512-bit FMA Peak Theoretical FLOPS Calculation
- **Affected Pages:** Page 18 (Ch 4.7), Page 40 (Q24), Verification Notes.
- **Initial Flawed Proposal:** Excel register stated "32 single-precision or 16 double-precision FLOPs per cycle" while Verification Notes stated 64 FP32 / 32 FP64.
- **Defect Identified:** Inconsistency between register and notes under the stated dual-unit core assumption. Each 512-bit FMA unit executes 16 FP32 elements $\times 2$ operations ($a \times b + c$) = 32 FP32 FLOPs/unit/cycle. A core with **dual 512-bit FMA units** delivers $32 \times 2 = \mathbf{64\text{ FP32 FLOPs/cycle}}$ and $16 \times 2 = \mathbf{32\text{ FP64 FLOPs/cycle}}$.
- **Reviewer Decision:** SYNCHRONIZED ACROSS ALL DELIVERABLES TO 64 FP32 / 32 FP64 UNDER DUAL-UNIT ASSUMPTION.
- **Approved Rewritten Formulation:** Define Peak Theoretical FLOPS: $\text{Peak FLOPS} = \text{Cores} \times f_\text{clock} \times \text{FLOPs/Cycle per Core}$. Under the stated dual 512-bit FMA unit assumption per core: each 512-bit FMA unit executes 16 FP32 elements $\times 2 = 32\text{ FP32 FLOPs/cycle}$, yielding **64 FP32 FLOPs/cycle** across dual units; for double-precision, 8 FP64 elements $\times 2 = 16\text{ FP64 FLOPs/cycle}$, yielding **32 FP64 FLOPs/cycle** across dual units.
- **Authoritative Standard:** IEEE Standard 754-2019 (Floating-Point Arithmetic) / SPEC CPU2017 Benchmark Rules.

### Item 3: NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2 & Degaussing / Media-Specific Sanitization
- **Affected Pages:** Page 34 (Ch 8.6), Page 42 (Q48), Page 44 (Q29), Page 45 (Bibliography).
- **Initial Flawed Proposal:** Listed degaussing as a physical "Destroy" method and implied that single-pass overwrite is universally sufficient for all storage media.
- **Defect Identified:**
  1. Under NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2, degaussing is classified under **Purge**, NOT Destroy. Furthermore, degaussing is strictly limited to suitable legacy magnetic media (tapes, magnetic HDDs) and is completely ineffective on solid-state flash media.
  2. Single-pass overwrite applies to magnetic media where logical LBAs map directly to fixed physical sectors. On solid-state drives (SSDs) with Flash Translation Layers (FTL), over-provisioning, and wear-leveling, logical overwrites cannot guarantee all unmapped physical flash blocks are cleared. SSDs require Purge via Cryptographic Erase (CE) or controller-native Sanitize block-erase.
- **Reviewer Decision:** REJECTED DEGAUSSING AS DESTROY; REWRITTEN TO DEFINE DEGAUSSING UNDER PURGE FOR MAGNETIC MEDIA ONLY; MANDATED MEDIA-SPECIFIC SANITIZATION.
- **Approved Rewritten Formulation:** Formally cite NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2 (Sept 2025) and IEEE 2883-2022. Mandate media-specific sanitization: (1) **Clear:** Logical sanitization using interface commands; single-pass overwrite is sufficient for magnetic media (HDDs), but is NOT universally sufficient for all SSDs/flash media due to unmapped wear-leveling blocks or over-provisioned areas; (2) **Purge:** Controller-level firmware sanitization making recovery infeasible even using laboratory tools: Cryptographic Erase (CE) or controller-native Sanitize block-erase (NVMe/ATA Sanitize) for flash storage. Note: **Degaussing is NOT a Destroy technique; it is a Purge method with limited use strictly for suitable legacy magnetic media** and is completely ineffective on flash storage; (3) **Destroy:** Physical destruction (shredding, incineration, disintegration). Distinguish technical Verification from organizational Validation.
- **Authoritative Standard:** NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2 (Guidelines for Media Sanitization, September 2025) / IEEE Std 2883-2022.

### Item 4: DNS RFC 9715 & 1232-Byte vs 1400-Byte Recommendations
- **Affected Pages:** Page 29 (Ch 7.4), Page 42 (Q46), Page 44 (Q27), Page 45 (Bibliography).
- **Initial Flawed Proposal:** Presented 1232 bytes as a rigid, universal fixed protocol limit or claimed it guarantees zero fragmentation across every valid network link, and omitted RFC 9715.
- **Defect Identified:** Presenting 1232 bytes as a rigid fixed protocol limit or claiming an absolute zero-fragmentation guarantee across all paths is inaccurate. Path MTU variations, tunneling (GRE/IPsec/VXLAN), or non-compliant links can still fragment packets. RFC 9715 (*IP Fragmentation Avoidance in DNS over UDP*, published January 2025 by Fujiwara & Vixie) explains that 1232 bytes is a **conservative fragmentation-avoidance recommendation** (derived from IPv6 minimum MTU of 1280B minus 40B IPv6 and 8B UDP headers), while 1400 bytes is a **contextual, practical resolver ceiling** deployed by major operators (such as Google Public DNS) to balance UDP payload efficiency against standard 1500B Ethernet MTUs with tunnel overhead. Neither is an immutable fixed protocol limit.
- **Reviewer Decision:** REJECTED ZERO-FRAGMENTATION GUARANTEE CLAIM; QUALIFIED 1232B AS A CONSERVATIVE RECOMMENDATION AND 1400B AS A CONTEXTUAL RESOLVER CEILING UNDER RFC 9715.
- **Approved Rewritten Formulation:** Detail that standard DNS uses UDP port 53. Clients advertise buffer capacity via EDNS0 (RFC 6891). Incorporate **RFC 9715 (January 2025)**: explain the operational relationship between the conservative 1232-byte recommendation (DNS Flag Day 2020, derived from IPv6 minimum MTU of 1280B minus 40B IPv6 and 8B UDP headers, serving as a conservative fragmentation-avoidance recommendation rather than an absolute zero-fragmentation guarantee) and the 1400-byte recommendation (RFC 9715, serving as a contextual resolver ceiling). Present neither as an immutable fixed protocol limit. When a DNS response exceeds the client's advertised buffer size, the server sets the Truncation flag (`TC=1`), signaling the resolver to fallback to **TCP port 53**.
- **Authoritative Standard:** RFC 9715 / RFC 6891 / DNS Flag Day 2020 / RFC 1035.

### Item 6: 12V-2x6 Power Connector Safety Qualification
- **Affected Pages:** Page 9 (Ch 2.6).
- **Initial Flawed Proposal:** Claimed that the 12V-2x6 connector completely eliminates thermal melting and fire risk.
- **Defect Identified:** Overstated safety guarantee. While 12V-2x6 (PCIe CEM 5.1 / ATX 3.1) reduces incomplete-insertion risk via recessed sense pins, it does NOT eliminate every thermal failure risk. Excessive cable bend radius, terminal pin misalignment, improper crimping, contact wear, or terminal contact resistance can still cause localized overheating under sustained 600W loads.
- **Reviewer Decision:** REJECTED ABSOLUTE SAFETY CLAIM; QUALIFIED INCOMPLETE-INSERTION RISK REDUCTION VS REMAINING THERMAL FAILURE RISKS.
- **Approved Rewritten Formulation:** Detail the ATX 3.1 / PCIe Base 6.0 (CEM 5.1) 12V-2x6 connector revision, which replaced the initial 12VHPWR design by shortening sideband sense pins (SENSE0 / SENSE1) by 1.7 mm and lengthening power terminals by 0.25 mm. This physical offset ensures that if the connector is not fully seated, the sense pins do not engage, prompting the power supply to restrict or disable high-wattage delivery. Qualify that while 12V-2x6 reduces incomplete-insertion risk, it does NOT eliminate every thermal failure risk: excessive cable bend radius near the plug, terminal pin misalignment, improper crimping, contact wear from high mating cycles, or terminal contact resistance can still induce localized high resistance and thermal degradation under sustained 600W loads.
- **Authoritative Standard:** PCI-SIG PCIe Card Electromechanical (CEM) Specification Revision 5.1 / Intel ATX 3.1 Design Guide.

### Item 7: DMA Bus Mastering as Architecture-Specific Example
- **Affected Pages:** Page 5 (Ch 1.5), Page 39 (Q3), Page 43 (Q5).
- **Initial Flawed Proposal:** Presented `HOLD`/`HLDA` bus tri-stating as a universal bus arbitration mechanism across all computer architectures.
- **Defect Identified:** `HOLD` and `HLDA` are specific to classical shared microprocessor buses (e.g., x86 8086/80286/ISA). Modern architectures (PCIe, ARM AMBA AXI) use packetized point-to-point split-transaction interconnects with IOMMUs rather than shared-bus tri-stating.
- **Reviewer Decision:** QUALIFIED HOLD/HLDA AS AN INSTRUCTIVE CLASSICAL MICROPROCESSOR EXAMPLE; CONTRASTED WITH PACKETIZED DMA.
- **Approved Rewritten Formulation:** Detail the hardware DMA bus mastering handshake as an instructive, architecture-specific bus arbitration example (common in classical x86/microprocessor buses): when an I/O peripheral requires high-speed bulk transfer, the DMA controller asserts the bus request line (HOLD) to the CPU; upon completing its current bus cycle, the CPU floats its address, data, and control lines into a high-impedance (tri-state) electrical condition and asserts Hold Acknowledge (HLDA), granting the DMA controller temporary exclusive direct control of the system bus. Contrast with modern high-performance interconnects (such as PCIe and ARM AMBA AXI) that utilize packetized point-to-point split-transaction protocols with I/O Memory Management Units (IOMMUs) rather than centralized shared-bus HOLD/HLDA lines.
- **Authoritative Standard:** Hennessy & Patterson: Computer Organization and Design / PCI-SIG PCIe Base Specification.

### Item 8: Display DAC & GPU Digital Signaling vs Panel Drivers
- **Affected Pages:** Page 4 (Ch 1.2).
- **Initial Flawed Proposal:** Overgeneralized display panel circuitry and claimed no DACs exist in modern displays.
- **Defect Identified:** While GPU-to-display transmission is purely digital (DisplayPort/TMDS/FRL without legacy RAMDACs), the display panel itself contains column/source driver ICs that convert digital pixel color values into analog drive voltages to charge liquid crystal subpixels (in LCDs) or modulate drive transistors (in OLEDs).
- **Reviewer Decision:** REWRITTEN TO CLEARLY SEPARATE GPU DIGITAL SIGNALING FROM INTERNAL PANEL DRIVER ANALOG VOLTAGE GENERATION.
- **Approved Rewritten Formulation:** Clearly distinguish GPU digital signaling from internal panel-driver circuitry: modern GPU display engines (DisplayPort Main Link, HDMI TMDS / Fixed Rate Link [FRL]) serialize digital framebuffers directly from VRAM across high-speed differential pairs to panel timing controllers (TCONs) without external DAC conversion (legacy RAMDACs were used for analog VGA). Inside the display panel itself, column/source driver ICs convert digital color values into analog drive voltages to charge liquid crystal subpixels (in LCDs) or modulate drive transistors (in OLEDs).
- **Authoritative Standard:** VESA DisplayPort Standard v2.1 / HDMI 2.1b Specification.

### Item 9: DDR5 Refresh Standards ($t_\text{REFW} = 32\text{ ms}$, $t_\text{REFI} = 3.9\text{ }\mu\text{s}$) & Density-Specific $t_\text{RFC}$
- **Affected Pages:** Page 12 (Ch 3.2), Page 39 (Q6), Page 43 (Q8), Page 45 (Bibliography).
- **Initial Flawed Proposal:** Cited legacy DDR4 values ($64\text{ ms} / 7.8\text{ }\mu\text{s}$) for DDR5 and made generalized $t_\text{RFC} > 400\text{ ns}$ claims without density qualification.
- **Defect Identified:** In DDR5 (JEDEC JESD79-5 Section 4.14 Table 169), the baseline 1x Refresh Window ($t_\text{REFW}$) is **32 ms** for normal operating temperatures ($T_\text{CASE} \le 85^\circ\text{C}$), requiring 8,192 refresh cycles, giving an Average Refresh Interval of **$t_\text{REFI} = 32\text{ ms} / 8192 = 3.90625\text{ }\mu\text{s} \approx 3.9\text{ }\mu\text{s}$** (halving in 2x mode to $t_\text{REFW} = 16\text{ ms}$ and $t_\text{REFI} = 1.95\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ at $85\text{--}95^\circ\text{C}$). Furthermore, $t_\text{RFC}$ is strictly die-density dependent per Table 170 (195 ns for 8Gb, 295 ns for 16Gb, 410 ns for 24Gb).
- **Reviewer Decision:** REPLACED WITH EXACT JEDEC JESD79-5 TABLE 169 & 170 DENSITY REFRESH VALUES; NOTED DDR4 CONTRAST.
- **Approved Rewritten Formulation:** In DDR5, the standard baseline 1x Refresh Window ($t_\text{REFW}$) is **32 ms** for normal operating temperatures ($T_\text{CASE} \le 85^\circ\text{C}$), requiring 8,192 refresh cycles, resulting in an Average Refresh Interval ($t_\text{REFI}$) of $32\text{ ms} / 8192 = 3.90625\text{ }\mu\text{s} \approx \mathbf{3.9\text{ }\mu\text{s}}$. In 2x Refresh mode at elevated temperatures ($85^\circ\text{C} < T_\text{CASE} \le 95^\circ\text{C}$), the refresh rate doubles, halving the window to $t_\text{REFW} = 16\text{ ms}$ and the interval to $t_\text{REFI} = 1.95\text{ }\mu\text{s}$. Note the legacy contrast with DDR4 ($t_\text{REFW} = 64\text{ ms}$ and $t_\text{REFI} = 7.8\text{ }\mu\text{s}$). Refresh Recovery Time ($t_\text{RFC}$) is not a universal constant, but is strictly specified per monolithic die density in JEDEC Table 170 (all-bank $t_\text{RFC1}$ is 195 ns for 8Gb, 295 ns for 16Gb, and 410 ns for 24Gb dies; same-bank $t_\text{RFCsb}$ is 115 ns for 8Gb, 130 ns for 16Gb, and 180 ns for 24Gb). Features like Same-Bank Refresh (SBR) and Per-Row Activation Counting (PRAC, JESD79-5C) mitigate refresh latency and Rowhammer vulnerability.
- **Authoritative Standard:** JEDEC Standard JESD79-5 / JESD79-5C Section 4.14 Table 169 & Table 170.

### Item 14: Removal of Claim that Power Button Drains PSU Capacitors
- **Affected Pages:** Page 37 (Ch 9.3 Mandate 3), Page 44 (Q18).
- **Initial Flawed Proposal:** Claimed that holding the power button discharges PSU bulk capacitors.
- **Defect Identified:** Holding the front power button closes the motherboard power header (`PWR_SW`), which signals the Super I/O chip to pull `PS_ON#` low. When AC mains power is disconnected, this may drain small residual charge on low-voltage secondary motherboard rails (+5VSB, +3.3V). However, it does **NOT** discharge the high-voltage primary bulk filter capacitors (300V–450V DC) inside the PSU enclosure, which remain galvanically isolated from secondary DC rails by switching transformers and primary rectification circuitry.
- **Reviewer Decision:** REMOVED INCORRECT CLAIM; CLARIFIED MOTHERBOARD SECONDARY RAIL DISSIPATION VS PRIMARY BULK CAPACITOR ISOLATION.
- **Approved Rewritten Formulation:** Disconnect the AC power cord and hold the front power button for 10-15 seconds to help dissipate residual energy on secondary motherboard rails; note that this does NOT guarantee discharge of the high-voltage primary bulk filter capacitors inside the PSU, which remain isolated from secondary circuits.
- **Authoritative Standard:** UL 62368-1 / IEC 62368-1 Audio/video, information and communication technology equipment - Safety requirements.

### Item 18: CMOS Dynamic Power Equation & Activity Factor Convention
- **Affected Pages:** Page 7 (Ch 2.1), Page 39 (Q14), Page 43 (Q6), Verification Notes.
- **Initial Flawed Proposal:** Used $P = \alpha \cdot C_L \cdot V_{DD}^2 \cdot f$ with ambiguous activity factor definitions, conflating $0 \to 1$ transition probability with total transition counts.
- **Defect Identified:** In CMOS logic, energy $E = C_L V_{DD}^2$ is drawn from the supply only on a $0 \to 1$ transition (charging load capacitance $C_L$). If $\alpha$ is defined as total transitions (both $0 \to 1$ and $1 \to 0$, where $\alpha_\text{total} = 2 \alpha_{0 \to 1}$), the equation requires a $1/2$ factor ($P = \frac{1}{2} \alpha C_L V_{DD}^2 f$). Using $P = \alpha C_L V_{DD}^2 f$ without strictly defining $\alpha$ as the $0 \to 1$ switching probability creates mathematical inconsistency.
- **Reviewer Decision:** STANDARDIZED CONVENTION ACROSS ALL DELIVERABLES: $\alpha$ STRICTLY DENOTES $0 \to 1$ TRANSITION PROBABILITY PER CLOCK CYCLE, WITH EXPLICIT RELATION TO THE ALTERNATIVE $1/2$ TOTAL TRANSITIONS CONVENTION.
- **Approved Rewritten Formulation:** Define CMOS dynamic switching power dissipation given by $P_\text{dynamic} = \alpha \cdot C_L \cdot V_{DD}^2 \cdot f$, where $\alpha$ is the switching activity factor, defined specifically as the probability that a power-consuming $0 \to 1$ logic transition occurs in a given clock cycle (drawing energy $E = C_L V_{DD}^2$ from the supply $V_{DD}$). Note: If activity is alternatively defined by counting total transitions (both $0 \to 1$ and $1 \to 0$), a $1/2$ factor applies ($P = \frac{1}{2} \alpha_\text{total} C_L V_{DD}^2 f$).
